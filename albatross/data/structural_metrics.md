# STRUCTURAL METRICS

> Albatross assesses the structural basis of the current silver rally. It does not forecast price direction, duration or profitability. The intent is to evaluate whether price moves are supported by structural, observable fundamentals, or primarily driven by sentiment, narrative, and leverage.

## INFLATION HEDGE
**Claim**: Silver is being bid as a protective hedge against rising inflation.

If silver is acting as an inflation hedge, price appreciation should align with:
- Rising inflation YoY.
- Worsening inflation expectations.
- Sustained upside surprises in CPI / PCE MoM releases.

The reality is:
- Inflation momentum is easing, not accelerating. U.S. annual inflation rate, as of December 2025, stands at 2.7%, down from September's 3.0%, and August's 2.9%.
- The silver rally is occurring *after* peak inflation fears. 2022 saw sustained inflation rates at > 7.0%.
- Directional mismatch between inflation trends and current silver prices.

**Silver is rallying whilst inflation is cooling. This is not consistent with the inflation hedge narrative.**

## DOLLAR DEBASEMENT
**Claim**: Silver is rallying as a hedge against USD debasement.

If silver is acting as a hedge against weakening USD, price appreciation should align with:
- Weakening DXY.
- Gold leading, silver following.

The reality is:
- DXY has remain range-bound over the relevant window (since June 2025).
- Silver, up 169.63% YTD, has materially outperformed gold, up 72.95% YTD.
- No disorderly USD behavior observed.

**DXY has remained flat while silver tripled. This is not consistent with the dollar hedge narrative.**

## GEOPOLITICAL RISK
**Claim**: Silver is being bid as a safe-haven asset amidst deteriorating geopolitical stress.

If silver is acting as a hedge against global instability:
- Silver should rally during peak stress events.

The reality is:
- June 13 ~ June 24, Iran-Israel 12-day War: silver saw no meaningful appreciation.
- February 24, 2022, Russia invasion of Ukraine: silver saw no meaningful appreciation.
- October 7, 2023, Hamas' surprise attack on Israel: silver saw no meaningful appreciation.

**Hot wars and geopolitical flare-ups historically fail to produce the same magnitude of silver response seen today. This is not consistent with the geopolitical hedge narrative.**

## MONETARY EASING
**Claim**: Anticipation of policy easing and rate cuts is supporting current silver prices.

If easing expectations are causal:
- Changes in expectations should precede silver price moves.
- Silver should respond consistently (inverse) to real yields shifts.

However:
- September 11, 10Y TIPS trading at 1.649.
- September 11, silver trading at $41.520/t.oz.
- December 26, 10Y TIPS trading at 1.886.
- December 26, silver trading at $79.110/t.oz.

Furthermore, changes in interest rate and prospects of further rate cuts from the FED have been telegraphed for months.

**Telegraphed easing and modest changes in yields fail to explain the timing and magnitude of the silver rally.**

## CENTRAL BANK BUYING
Reports suggest that central banks have begun taking an interest in silver. For context:
- The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) has reported purchases of 932,000 shares of iShares Silver Trust (SLV) and 203,700 shares of Global X Silver Miners ETF (SIL), totalling $40.6M USD.
- Russia's 2025 - 2027 draft federal budget allocates $535M USD for the acquisition of private metals including gold, platinum, palladium, and for the first time, silver.
- Reserve Bank of India (RBI) now allows silver as collateral for loans.

It is important to note, however:
- Globally, an average >$5bn USD worth of silver is traded daily. This figure has peaked to >$15bn USD as of December 2025. With a market cap of $4.5T USD, Saudi Arabia's buy is less than 0.001% of silver's current valuation.
- Russia's $535M USD federal budget is **not** earmarked specifically for silver. Russia has yet to disclose purchase volumes, balance sheets showing silver holdings, IMF reserve line items, or official CBR statements. Allocation may vary greatly.
- Reserve Bank of India has yet to disclose silver reserves, IMF reporting, balance sheet changes, purchases, or intent statements.

**While central banks are participating, their scale is trivial relative to the global silver market, and cannot account for magnitude of the current rally.**

## THE CHINA EQUATION
Beijing, as well, has played an understated role in silver's current rally.

China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) reports: starting January 1st, 2026, only large, state-certified companies producing at least 80 tonnes of silver annually will be granted access to state-controlled export licenses. Other requirements include: credit lines of at least ~ RMB 200M+, ISO certifications, export history, and safety/environment compliance. Effectively, this measure restricts smaller players from accessing the global market, establishes a de facto quota system, curbing exports, and consolidating the domestic sector. The extent of this reduction is yet to be determined, and it is imperative the following be understood: China is **not** banning exports of silver. China is selectively permitting who or what is legally allowed to export silver. Actual export values may vary greatly.

Furthermore, supply of physical silver available for delivery / settlement in China's Shanghai Future Exchange (SHFE) warehouse is reported to be low. As of 26 December 2025, official figures report 852.417 tons remaining.

It is important to note, however, that supply of physical silver available in China's SHFE warehouse does **not** represent **total** available, domestic supply. Most physical silver in China is illiquid, off-exchange, or politically/industrially sticky. SHFE figures do not represent total silver mined in China; total silver held by refiners, fabricators, and industrial firms; silver in jewelry supply chains; silver sitting in vaults, SOEs, and strategic stockpiles; or silver already committed to industrial processes. In essence, Shanghai Futures Exchange warehouse stockpile figures are a small subset of China's silver supply. Real supplies may be many orders of magnitude more than reported.

SHFE warehouse supply of silver has also been reported at <900 tons previously. July 2024 saw stockpiles similarly at 600 ~ 750 tons, and spot silver saw no meaningful price appreciation then.

**China's policy changes do not constitute a fundamental shock sufficient to justify the current rally; supply remains largely unchanged, under administrative control, and can be adjusted at Beijing's discretion.**

> A final note: China is both importer (user) and exporter (seller) of silver. While miners benefit from high silver prices due to export revenue, producers suffer due to rising input COGS. Beijing and the CCP administrative has incentive to maintain stability within their domestic manufacturing sector by increasing / decreasing available supply of physical silver for export, and / or altering current policies accordingly.
